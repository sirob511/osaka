Decision
Give the lantern ceremony a dedicated page and navigation link. It has a documented local history, cultural meaning, and practical attendance information. Describe it as a ceremony or observance of remembrance and peace, not one fixed national holiday or a generic light show.

Editorial and visual cautions
The user-supplied Culture Trip article returned 403:
https://theculturetrip.com/asia/japan/articles/the-history-of-toro-nagashi-japans-glowing-lantern-festival
Do not repeat its reported blanket claim that Toro Nagashi began in 1946. Distinguish the older tradition from the histories of individual modern ceremonies. Do not import Hiroshima's lantern prices, materials, registration procedures, or river-release instructions into Chicago guidance. The local event page is the authority for attendance rules. Explain the lantern's message, paper enclosure, light, and floating base as an illustration of the tradition; materials and ceremony procedure depend on the organizers.

No explicitly reusable photograph of the Chicago ceremony was verified. Organizer photos are not licensed merely because they are publicly visible. Original explanatory artwork must be identified as illustration, not documentary coverage of an event. Existing garden photography shows the setting only. No organizers were contacted.

Avoid calling the garden’s 2023 inaugural event Chicago’s first-ever lantern ceremony.
